Where things land. A translucent preview follows the cursor and shows exactly what a click will write — the white cage marks the cells, the dark patch is its shadow on the ground. Clicking open ground places on the ground under the cursor (side to side picks the column, near to far picks the layer). Clicking a FACE of an existing block attaches against that face.

Pasting a piece. The cyan cell in the preview is the piece's attachment point — the cell that lands on whatever you pointed at. Which side of the piece that is follows the face you click: click a top face and the piece's bottom rests on it, a left face and its right side butts against it, and so on. Blank margin around a copied selection is ignored, so a piece always lands flush.

Turning a piece. R turns it flat — in the horizontal plane about a vertical axis, so a wall facing the ballista becomes one running away from it. T tips it over within its layer, so a tall column becomes a long horizontal run. Shift with either to turn the other way. Ninety degrees per press, and each axis keeps going all the way round — four presses is a full turn back to where you started, and the two axes combine, so any orientation is reachable. It turns about the cyan attachment cell, so the piece swings around the spot it's anchored to instead of drifting off it. The Paste button shows the current size as columns×rows×layers plus how far it's turned (a symmetric piece looks the same at 0° and 180°, so the angle is the only way to tell).

Letters and flags are generated from js/data.js's MAT table above — this panel used to be the only documentation and could rot the moment a material changed. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, Ctrl+C/Ctrl+V copy and paste a selection, Ctrl+Z/Ctrl+Y undo/redo, Esc cancels a tool. This tool never runs the real game's camera, input or scan lists — mouse/keyboard only, on purpose.
